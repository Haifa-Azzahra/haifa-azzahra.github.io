Improving IELTS Writing with Focus

A full-cycle Self-Regulated Learning experience, designed from Zimmerman’s SRL framework outward, using a two-tool ecosystem where Storyline manages the session architecture and an AI writing coach provides adaptive, essay-specific feedback.

Tools
Storyline 3 · Google AI Studio · JS · Sheets
Audience
Band 6.5 IELTS learners (independent study)
Duration
1–3 hours per session
Format
Self-paced interactive module
Published
April 2026
📋

Note: This is a conceptual project designed for this portfolio. The design, architecture, and evaluation framework are fully realised; the Storyline module and AI coach are functional prototypes, not deployed or validated products.

The Problem

Test Pressure

A high-pressure test that requires the right strategy

  • No single correct answer; judged across four interdependent criteria
  • 60-minute time limit, with Task 2 weighted double
  • Without a strategy, performance stays inconsistent under pressure
Access / Cost

Financial cost barriers

  • Many learners prepare independently due to cost barriers
  • Without structure, practice becomes inconsistent and poorly targeted
  • Repeated attempts compound financial strain and erode confidence

Design Question

How can you provide a self-directed learner with the structure of a coached session: without a coach, without an LMS, and without a large budget?

Learning Needs Analysis

The core gap here is not a lack of knowledge of IELTS criteria. Many Band 6.5 learners can identify the assessment criteria, yet still struggle to apply them consistently under test conditions.

Learner Condition

What is happening to the learner?

Can identify IELTS assessment criteria, yet struggles to apply them consistently under real test conditions.

→
The Centerpiece

Self-Regulated Learning

Grounded in Zimmerman’s SRL framework: three phases (Forethought, Performance, Reflection), practiced as a cycle rather than taught as content.

→
Learner Impact

What does this affect?

Strengthens goal-setting, reflection accuracy, and metacognitive calibration; shifts learners from passive practice to intentional, data-informed learning.

The Three-Phase Design

Rather than explaining what SRL is, every scene in the module requires learners to do what self-regulated learners do.

🔮
Phase 1

Forethought

Band gap analysis, scaffolded goal-setting with sentence stems, motivational grounding, and anxiety reframing. Learners identify their own gap before setting a target.

🛠
Phase 2

Performance

Learners submit a full essay to the AI writing coach. They receive their primary gap, a sample of improved text, and targeted practice suggestions.

🔄
Phase 3

Reflection

Three sequential cognitive moves: evaluate accurately, decide adaptively, commit specifically. Every prompt targets a specific self-regulatory behaviour with a theoretical basis.

The Learning Ecosystem

Two learner-facing products carry the experience. Storyline manages the session architecture; the AI coach provides adaptive, essay-specific feedback.

📊

JavaScript + Google Apps Script

Chosen as the evaluation layer for LMS-free, zero-cost deployment. A Google Apps Script Web App receives all 27 session variables from Storyline, writing each completed session as a new row.

📋

Google Sheets

The evaluation backend. Each completed session writes one timestamped, learner-named row, with all Forethought, Performance, and Reflection variables stored in structured columns.

Learning Evaluation

Core question: Does repeated use of this tool build self-regulation in IELTS writing learners over time?

Designed to Evaluate
  • Goal quality: does smart_goal_action become more specific, and smart_goal_weeks more realistic?
  • Anxiety & self-efficacy trajectory: does the tone of struggle_reframe and self_efficacy become more confident over sessions?
  • Engagement: session_count tracks how many full cycles each learner completes; drop-off rate indicates where motivation breaks down.
Designed to Evaluate
  • Self-diagnosis accuracy: does criterion_focus start matching gap_identified more often? Mismatches are expected early; convergence is expected over time.
Designed to Evaluate
  • Reflection depth: do improvement_attribution responses shift from surface (“I did better”) to causal (“I improved because I focused on topic sentences”)?
Designed to Evaluate
  • Adaptive behaviour: does new_criterion_focus show variety across sessions, or does the learner stay stuck on one criterion?

Also tracked across sessions: correlation between session_count and current_band_score. Improvement here is defined not only as higher band scores, but as better regulation of the writing process itself.

Reflection

The pivotal moment was refining the approach from teaching about SRL to designing through it. During the course outline matrix development, every scene was reframed: does this prompt action or reflection, or just comprehension? That single question restructured the entire learning architecture.

The current version provides one practice prompt per session. A production version would expand prompt variety and invest further in the Storyline UI/UX: developing a visual language that reinforces self-regulation, calm, focused, and intentionally unhurried. The interface should signal that this is a space for reflection, not speed. The goal would also be better visual cohesion between the Storyline module and the AI writing coach so they feel like one ecosystem rather than two adjacent tools.

This learning experience builds the internal process of self-regulation. But self-regulation doesn’t mean learning alone: writing improves through feedback, diverse models, and engagement with a learning community. This tool is one part of a larger ecosystem. It doesn’t replace the external environment, and it was never designed to.

Learning Objective

By the end of repeated sessions, learners will independently regulate their IELTS Writing practice: setting evidence-based goals, monitoring strategy use, and reflecting accurately to drive improvement toward Band 7+.

Design Frameworks
🧠Zimmerman’s SRL Model: three-phase session architecture
🎯Gollwitzer’s Implementation Intentions: if-then goal planning in the Reflection phase
⚡Cognitive Load Theory: sentence stem scaffolding, one-concept-per-scene structure
💬Motivational Interviewing: self-generated reasons displayed back to strengthen commitment
